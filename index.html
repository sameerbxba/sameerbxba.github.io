How I work a process
Walk it Sit with the people doing the work. The documented process and the real one are never the same.
Measure it Volumes, aging, rework. Numbers instead of impressions, so priorities aren't guesses.
Design it Rules for the routine, judgment kept human, and controls that still hold.

I started in Computer Engineering, switched to Electrical after second year, and somewhere across five co-op terms found that the part I kept gravitating toward wasn't the hardware. 03 / Things I built
GenAIPersonal build2026

SyncBase

A tool that reads across a project's documents and flags where teams are working from different assumptions — the kind of quiet mismatch that turns into rework three weeks later. Built to test my own thinking about where GenAI genuinely fits in operations work.

What it taught me: the model was the easy part. The hard part was deciding which of fifty flags were the three that mattered. Precision over recall, because a tool that flags everything gets ignored.
ServiceNowProcess redesignEquitable Life

Approval workflow redesign

Approvals were slow and the conversation was about how much to automate. Two weeks of shadowing showed that a meaningful share involved judgment calls users didn't want automated away. So I categorized approvals into rule-based versus judgment with rough volumes on each, and proposed the split: routing rules for the rule-based ones, a structured review queue for the rest.

Why it worked: the users adopted it without resistance, because they kept oversight exactly where it mattered to them.
BPMNSalesforceSeaspan

Warranty process, end to end

Seven departments, each with its own intake process, and nobody able to see the whole flow. I ran discovery across all seven and mapped case, warranty, approval, and escalation end to end, then translated it into the requirements the build ran on and coordinated testing through go-live.

The real result: teams who only knew their own step started using the map as the version everyone worked from.
